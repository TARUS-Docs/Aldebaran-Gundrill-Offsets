Step 1 Foundation

Pre-Cal Check: Mechanical Backlash

Perform this check before altering any drive ratios. If there is uncompensated mechanical slop, linear resolution calibration will produce inconsistent results.

Tools Needed

  • • Magnetic base and a high-resolution dial indicator (capable of reading 0.0001 inches or 0.001 mm).
1

Set Up the Indicator

Mount the magnetic base securely to the spindle housing, ram, or machine head. Position the indicator stylus against a clean, rigid surface that moves with the opposing machine element (like a 1-2-3 block bolted to the table or a solid fixture). Ensure the stylus is perfectly parallel to the axis of travel to prevent cosine error.

2

Pre-load the Indicator (Critical Step)

Using the handwheel, jog the axis in one direction (e.g., Positive) until the indicator needle rotates at least one full revolution. This ensures the ball nut is fully engaged against the screw threads in that direction.

3

Establish Zero

Without reversing direction, carefully zero the face of the dial indicator. Next, zero the relative position (or distance-to-go) coordinate on the machine control.

4

Move Forward

Continue moving the axis in the exact same direction (Positive) for a short, specific distance (e.g., 0.100 inches or 2.0 mm) using the handwheel or MDI.

5

Reverse to Zero

Reverse your direction (Negative) and slowly feed the axis back until the machine control display reads exactly 0.0000 again.

6

Record the Backlash

Look at the dial indicator. It should theoretically return to exactly zero. If the needle falls short of zero, that difference is your mechanical backlash (lost motion).

Example: If the control reads zero, but the indicator shows 0.0015 inches remaining, the axis has 0.0015 inches of backlash.
7

Verify Consistency

Repeat this forward-and-back test at least three times. If the backlash number is consistent, it must be compensated for in the machine parameters before proceeding. If the number fluctuates wildly, inspect the machine for loose thrust bearings, a loose encoder coupling, or mechanical binding.

Next Step: Handoff

Once the axis mechanics are confirmed tight and backlash is documented/compensated, you may proceed to scale the axis in Linear Resolution.

Step 2 Calibration

Linear Resolution

This procedure calculates and applies the correct electronic gear ratio so that when the machine is told to move 1 inch, it physically moves precisely 1 inch. This must be verified for the X, Y, Z, and Drill axes.

Tools Needed

  • • Magnetic base and high-resolution dial indicator (capable of reading 0.0010 inches or 0.0254 mm).
  • • Known Precision Length Standards (See Calibration Dept. for kit).
  • • Long Gundrill spindle plug (Used in the GD chuck to extend indicator reach for Drill Resolution).
  • • Indicator Holder Articulating Arm.
1

Record the Current Ratio

Before making any movements, navigate to Axis Mechanics / Scaling in the drive parameters. Record the exact Current Input Ratio and Current Output Ratio.

2

Measure Physical vs. Control Distance

Move the axis along your measurement standard (laser, artifact, or precision length standards). Avoid cosine error by ensuring standards are perfectly parallel to the axis of travel.

  • Record the exact distance the machine control says it moved. (Control Measured Distance)
  • Record the exact physical distance measured by your standard. (Standard Actual Distance)
3

Calculate the New Ratio

Use the interactive calculator below to perform the precise floating-point math required to generate the exact gear ratio.

Interactive Scaling Calculator

Enter your measurements. The calculator uses high-precision math to output the exact whole number ratio needed for Bosch drives.

4

Update Ratio (Control Specific)

A Digital (Q Proxy / Bosch)
  1. Disable drives. Switch to Parameter Mode (PM).
  2. Navigate to Axis Mechanics / Scaling in IndraWorks.
  3. Enter the calculated whole number into the Input Ratio field. Do not alter Output Ratio.
  4. Switch back to Operating Mode (OM).
  5. Reset Absolute Encoder Position to establish new baseline. Clear faults.
B Analog (Mill Proxy)
  1. Navigate to Service parameters in the Aldebaran UI.
  2. Locate the axis resolution scaling parameter.
  3. Apply scaling factor and save.
  4. Power cycle the control to ensure scaling is fully applied across coordinate systems.
5

Enable Drives and Verify

Turn the drives back on and reset your preset/work offset. Perform one final physical measurement against your standard to verify the machine resolution is now perfectly matched to the physical distance.

Troubleshooting Resolution Inconsistencies

If the machine fails to land on the correct dimension after applying the new Bosch gear ratio, check the following common issues before recalculating.

1. Procedural & Math Errors
  • Math Reversal: Ensure you divided Control Measured by Standard Actual. Reversing this will double your error.
  • Dropped Zeros: Verify the Output Ratio was not accidentally modified (e.g. missing a zero).
  • Failure to Reset Absolute Position: Ensure the coordinate system was reset after switching back to Operating Mode.
2. Mechanical & Hardware Issues
  • Backlash (Lost Motion): Are you measuring from different directions? Always approach start/end points moving in the exact same direction.
  • Loose Encoder Coupling: If measurements are completely erratic on a motor-mounted encoder, the coupling may be slipping.
  • Dirty/Misaligned Scales: Clean linear scales and verify read-head LED status to ensure counts aren't dropping.
3. Measurement Setup Errors
  • Cosine Error: Is your standard perfectly parallel? If skewed, the machine travels a hypotenuse (always longer). Indicate your standard first.
  • Thermal Expansion: Calibrating a "hot" machine results in inaccurate ratios when it cools down.

Next Step: Handoff

With linear X/Y/Z/Drill resolution verified, ensure the B and A axes are accurately scaled. Proceed to Rotary/Tilt Resolution.

Step 3 Calibration

Rotary & Tilt Axis Resolution

For machines equipped with Rotary (B) and/or Tilt (A) axes, scaling must be mathematically verified to ensure exact angular positioning.

Tools Needed

  • • Mag base & high-resolution indicator
  • • Indicator Holder Articulating Arm
  • • Precision Block (clamped) or Shoulder Bolt
  • • Precision Rod Cat 50 holder
  • • Graphite Z Tube with Metal feet (Tripod)
  • • 10mm Tooling Ball & Holder

Part 1: Rotary Table Resolution

If the machine lacks a Rotary Table, skip to Part 2.

1

Prerequisite: Verify Squareness

Make sure the "Tilt" axis is physically square before gathering this info. Check with a ceramic square by jogging the Y-axis and tramming the spindle with tilt set at 0°. If the machine is not square in Y, STOP. Escalate to supervisor before proceeding.

2

Sweep Standard at 0°

Command the Rotary Table to exactly 0°. Indicate a precision straight edge (or shoulder bolt mounted in a fixture hole) parallel to the machine axis travel.

3

Rotate 360° and Verify

Command the table to rotate a full 360° back to the starting point. Check the exact same surface to see if it is perfectly parallel with the original reading.

4

Calculate Error and Correct

If the reading has drifted, jog the rotary table until the straight edge is parallel again. Look at the rotary axis readout on the screen to determine the angular error. Adjust the Rotary Resolution scaling in the Service parameters to eliminate this mathematical drift.

Part 2: Tilt Axis Resolution

If the machine lacks a Tilt head, skip to Next Step.

5

Determine Tool Length to Center of Ball

  • Put the 10mm tooling ball in the Auxiliary spindle. Indicate it to remove any run-out.
  • Pick up the high spot at the end of the tooling ball with an indicator and write down the Z Machine position.
  • Move over, touch the indicator to the Spindle Face, and record the Z Machine position again.
  • Subtract the 2 numbers for overall length. Finally, subtract the radius of the tooling ball. This is your precise Tool Length.
6

Collect 5-Point Angular Data

Set up a tripod indicator on the table high enough/close enough to reach at 0°, +15°, and -15° Tilt angles.

  • With tilt at 0°, move the tooling ball to 0,0,0 on the tripod stand. Record machine Y, Z, and Tilt.
  • Repeat this exactly for -15°, -7.5°, +7.5°, and +15°.
7

Calculate & Apply Offsets

Enter these 5 data sets (along with Tool Length and Rotary Table Diameter) into the Tarus Calibration Calculator. The calculator will output the values needed to set tilt resolution and the distance from spindle face to the pivot point. Enter these into Service.

Note: Before adjusting the actual resolution scaling, try entering just the calculated pivot distances, re-collect the Y/Z values for all 5 angles, and rerun the calculator. Resolution error is often corrected entirely by fixing the pivot distance.

Verify via Tool Vector

  • Ensure tool length/offsets are active, and turn on Tool Vector mode.
  • At Tilt = 0°, set tooling ball to 0 on all 3 tripod indicators. Preset X, Y, Z to 0 on control.
  • Back tooling ball away. Tilt to one of the checked angles (e.g., +15°).
  • Execute machine back to X=0, Y=0, Z=0. (Ensure it will not crash into tripod).
  • The tripod indicators should read no more than .002" from zero. Repeat for all angles.

Next Step: Handoff

Once distances and rotational angles are scaled properly, proceed to check the geometric orthogonality in Linear Squareness.

Step 4 Calibration

Linear Squareness

Ensuring orthogonal geometry across the X, Y, and Z axes. This must be done AFTER linear scaling, otherwise geometric measurements over distance will be inherently flawed.

Tools Needed

  • • Mag base & high-resolution indicator
  • • Indicator Holder Articulating Arm
  • • Marker
  • • Precision Machine Square (Ceramic or Steel). Must be error verified/inspected by Calibration Dept. Do not use if bumped.
Kinematics Reminder (Right Hand Rule): Tarus Gundrills are Horizontal machines. All checks below assume standard Cartesian coordinates: X-Axis travels Left/Right, Y-Axis travels Up/Down, and Z-Axis travels In/Out (towards the operator/table).
1

XZ Squareness Plane (Table Top)

  • Place the precision square flat on the table. Mount the indicator in the Aux Spindle.
  • Indicate one edge of the square parallel to the X-axis (Left/Right). Jog X and tap the square until TIR is exactly zero along its length. Clamp carefully.
  • Without moving the square, position the indicator against the perpendicular edge.
  • Sweep the Z-axis (In/Out) along this edge. Record the deviation. This maps the XZ Squareness (Yaw).
2

XY Squareness Plane (Front Face)

  • Stand the precision square vertically on the table, facing the spindle.
  • Indicate the bottom side of the square parallel to the X-axis (Left/Right). Jog X and adjust the square until TIR is zero. Clamp securely.
  • Position the indicator against the vertical face of the square.
  • Sweep the Y-axis (Up/Down) up and down the face. Record the deviation. This maps the XY Squareness.
3

YZ Squareness Plane (Side Face)

  • Keep the square standing vertically, but rotate it 90 degrees so it faces sideways (towards X travel).
  • Indicate the bottom side of the square parallel to the Z-axis (In/Out). Jog Z and adjust until TIR is zero. Clamp securely.
  • Position the indicator against the vertical face of the square.
  • Sweep the Y-axis (Up/Down) up and down the face. Record the deviation. This maps the YZ Squareness (Pitch).
4

Apply Compensation

Once all three planes (XY, YZ, XZ) have been mapped and recorded, these values must be entered into the Aldebaran Compensation matrix to mathematically true the machine's orthogonal movements. (Detailed Aldebaran UI path pending).

Next Step: Handoff

Once the linear axes are verified for backlash, resolution, and squareness, the machine foundation is sound. Proceed to align the spindle mechanics in Gundrill Spindle Tram.

Step 5 Foundation

Gundrill Spindle Tram

This procedure aligns the Gundrill throughout its entire length, forcing the tool to drill straight. It is completely independent of Aux spindle accuracy.

Tools Needed

  • • Gundrill Spindle Plug w/ set screw
  • • Precision Rod
  • • Rear Chipbox Bearing
  • • Front Nose Bushing
  • • 2 Interapid Indicators & Mag Bases
  • • Allen wrench set (Inch/Metric)
  • • 1ft pipe (for breaking bolts free)
1

Check Gundrill Nose Face

Jog Tilt axis to exactly Zero. Remove the nose adapter if mounted. Jog the indicator along the face in X/Y.

Acceptance Criteria: Within .0005 total error. (If greater, Tilt Home Offset must be adjusted).

2

Tram I.D. of Bearing

With the plug in the GD Spindle and indicator mounted, tram the I.D of the Bearing. To adjust, loosen mounting screws (leave 1 tight to pivot). Use a brass drift to tap and correct up/down & left/right error.

Acceptance Criteria: Under .0005 total error.

3

Check Face of Bearing

Check the face of the bearing. If error is excessive, shim stock may be needed, or remove the bearing to stone & clean behind it.

Acceptance Criteria: Within .0010 total error.

Pro-Tip: The bearing might have been taper ground from the factory. Use a paint marker to identify the "top" of the bearing to ensure consistent orientation when remounting.
4

Install Bushings & Inspection Rod

Install the Rear Chipbox Bushing & Front nose bushing. Insert the Inspection Rod into both and split the length overhang difference equally from the front/rear.

5

Final Verification Sweep

Set up 2 indicators tight to the bushing (Zero at Top & Side). Check for error by jogging the Z axis. If error exists, adjust the Nose Piece by loosening bolts (leaving 1 tight to pivot).

CRITICAL: You MUST jog the machine in the Z axis to inspect this. Moving the rod by hand is incorrect. Adjust until Top & Side read zero both in tight, and out far.

Next Step: Handoff

Once the Gundrill is mechanically aligned along its length, you must ensure its face is perfectly square to the table. Proceed to Tilt Axis Home Offset.

Step 6 Calibration

Tilt Axis Home Offset

Squares up the face of the Gundrill Nose and the Aux spindle perpendicular to the work piece and table. Vital for preventing steps or tapers during face milling operations.

1

Mechanical Leveling & Sweep Setup

  • Install precision bar in main spindle. Use torpedo level to jog Tilt axis until approximately horizontal (rough zero).
  • Remove precision bar. Install collet holder with test indicator ("worm" arm) oriented 90° to spindle centerline.
  • Place machinist square on table, face towards spindle.
2

Measure Jog Error

  • Preload indicator against square (-Z direction). Manually rotate spindle 180° ensuring full contact.
  • Find highest (+Y) and lowest (-Y) points of indicator arc. Mark these two horizontal lines on the square.
  • Jog Y-axis to bottom line. Jog Z to touch square and Zero dial.
  • Jog Y-axis straight up to top line. Record this value (Jog Error).
3

Sweep & Tilt Correction

  • Without jogging axes, manually rotate spindle to sweep between the top and bottom marks. Note the TIR (Sweep Error).
  • Adjust Tilt axis in small increments until Sweep Error exactly matches your recorded Jog Error.
  • The axis is now physically at true zero. Do not move it.
4

Set Home Offset (Control Specific)

You must command the drive to accept the current position as permanent zero. The steps differ based on the machine's control type.

A Digital (Q Proxy / Bosch)
  1. End Qproxy service and close Aldebaran.
  2. Launch IndraWorks, connect to Tilt drive. Enter Service Mode (P0-0002).
  3. Find S-0-0402. Enter 10 (Set abs position) and execute.
  4. Find S-0-0192. Enter 1 (Save all params) and execute.
B Analog (Mill Proxy)
  1. Open the Aldebaran service interface on the controller.
  2. Navigate to: Service > Axis > Tilt > Limits (or Home Offset).
  3. Adjust the Home Offset value by the measured error amount to zero the axis.
  4. Save the configuration and Power Cycle the control to apply.

Next Step: Handoff

Now that the spindles are physically square to the table, you must map the exact dimensional distance between them. Proceed to Aux Spindle Offsets.

Step 7 Calculation

Aux Spindle Offsets

Maps out the offsets needed for accuracy from the Aux spindle to the Gundrill. This ensures both spindles share exactly the same coordinates for multi-operation routines.

Prerequisite Check

Tram and Tilt Axis Home Offset must be verified before proceeding. Any changes to them will invalidate these offsets. Tool length should be forced to zero (or 0.000001 if zero is rejected) for all steps below.

Z

Z-Axis Offset

Path: Service > Heads > Gundrill > Pivot Z
  • Insert Cat50 holder into extended Aux Spindle. Touch indicator to Aux Spindle Flange, preload by 0.010", and Preset Z-axis to zero.
  • Insert Nose Bushing/dowel into GD Spindle. Touch indicator to face of GD spindle, jog Z until preload matches 0.010".
  • Record Z readout value. Enter into service parameters and verify.
Y

Y-Axis Offset

Path: Service > Heads > Gundrill > Pivot Y
  • Touch indicator to bottom of Aux Spindle dowel. Sweep X left/right to find high point. Preload 0.010" and Preset Y-axis to zero.
  • Move indicator to bottom of GD Spindle dowel. Sweep to high point, jog Y until preload matches 0.010".
  • Record Y readout value. Enter into service parameters and verify.
X

X-Axis Offset

Path: Service > Heads > Gundrill > Spindle X
  • Touch indicator to side of Aux Spindle dowel. Sweep Y up/down to find high point. Preload 0.010" and Preset X-axis to zero.
  • Move indicator to side of GD Spindle dowel. Sweep to high point, jog X until preload matches 0.010".
  • Record X readout value. Enter into service parameters and verify.

Next Step: Handoff

With the spindles mapped, the machine's core geometry is set. If this machine utilizes 4-axis operations, proceed to Rotary Table Centers. If not, jump to Toolbump Calibration.

Step 8 Calibration

Offset Rotary Table Centers

Allows a block of material to be setup on one side and then rotated to the other 3 sides for machining without removing the workpiece. Finding absolute center is highly dependent on Tram, Tilt, and Spindle Offsets being spot on.

Prerequisite Check

Spindle Offsets must be verified and correct before continuing.

1

Determine Tool Length

  • Create a "Flat End Mill" in Tool Manager. Pick up high spot on end of Edge Finder with indicator (preload .0100). Record Z Machine position.
  • Move over and touch indicator (at .0100 preload) directly to Spindle Face. Record Z Machine position again.
  • Subtract the values to find total tool length. Update Tool Selector.
2

Find X Table Center Position

  • Set straight edge on table at Rotary 0°. Pick up edge in X axis with Edge Finder. Record First X Position.
  • Rotate table 180° and pick up the exact same edge. Record Second X Position.
  • Add the two numbers and divide by 2. This is the X Table Center. Enter this into Service > Axis > X Axis > Limits.
3

Find Z Table Center Position

  • Rotate table to 90° or 270° (part face facing you). Touch edge finder to face. Record Z Machine Position.
  • Subtract the two X Machine Positions (from Step 2) and divide by 2 (distance of surface to center).
  • Add: (Distance to center) + (Edge finder length) + (Z Flange Offset) + (Z Machine Position) = Z Table Center. Enter into Service > Axis > Z Axis > Limits.

CRITICAL POST-PROCEDURE

Any changes made during the alignment phase necessitates a final toolbump reset to guarantee safe tool loads. You MUST immediately perform a Toolbump Calibration.

Step 9 Calibration

Toolbump Calibration

The final step to correctly calibrating the machine and turning over an accurate machine to the customer. Techs must complete this whenever ANY of the other procedures are tweaked.

Prerequisites & Setup

  • Ensure Rotary and Tilt axes are set to Zero.
  • Use a Twist Drill or Flat End Mill. No radius tooling.
  • Note: If performed right after Offsets/Tilt calibration, a Cat50 with dowel or Long Edge Finder is fine, provided length was manually measured and is perfectly accurate.
1

Measure & Update Tool Rack

Insert tool into Aux spindle. Manually measure its exact length and enter this value into the Tool Rack for the specific tool being used.

2

Position Tool over Switch

Center tool over Tool Bump in X/Y axes. Retract Z-axis to desired starting height (ensure height clears the longest expected tool).

3

Run Calibration Routine

  • Navigate: File > Exit to Service > Devices > Tool Bumps.
  • Verify your manual tool length appears correctly in the field.
  • Click Calibrate Bump and Store Position. Tool will move and touch switch.
  • Click Apply, then Save (Floppy Disk icon).

Final Verification

Return to main user interface. Run a standard automated tool bump routine. Result must be within .002" of manual length.

Next Step: Handoff

Toolbump is calibrated. Proceed to Probe Calibration if applicable to finalize the machine setup.
